## What to know about this topic

> [!note] The largest topic in the paper, and the most predictable
> 119 pages of 9618 Legend — more than any other topic. Databases appears as a **whole multi-part question in every paper since 2021**, and it is built the same way each time:
> **scenario → table definitions → E-R diagram → terminology → normalisation → SQL.**
> The later parts depend on reading the table definitions correctly in the first, so work them in order.

> [!info] Indexing — named in the syllabus, examined zero times
> Every other term in the terminology list (entity, tuple, attribute, all four key types, all three relationship degrees, referential integrity) has been examined repeatedly. **Indexing has never appeared in a question in either series.**

> [!info] AVG — the same story in 8.3
> The syllabus names `SUM`, `COUNT` **and `AVG`**. SUM and COUNT appear in almost every paper; **`AVG` has appeared zero times** in either series. `ORDER BY` appeared for the first time ever in w25.

> [!warning] "Are, **or are not**, in 3NF"
> 9618 has asked this twice — and **both times the answer was that the tables *are* in 3NF**. The harder half of the bullet has no 9618 precedent, so the model answers for it come from 9608.

> [!danger] Two syllabus bullets have never been examined at all
> **8.3.1 (DDL)** and **8.3.2 (DML)** have **completely empty pages** in the Legend. They are tested only indirectly, through the SQL questions.
